Location

Trimbakeshwar is located near Nashik in Maharashtra, at the source of the sacred Godavari River. The temple is situated at the base of the Brahmagiri mountain in a picturesque natural setting.

History

The Trimbakeshwar Temple is dedicated to Lord Shiva in the form of Trimbakeshwar (Lord of three deities). According to mythology, the temple was established by Brahmin Rishi Gautama, who sought to atone for a curse. The name "Trimbakeshwar" comes from the three peaks of the Brahmagiri mountain near the temple. The temple is famous for housing the origin point of the sacred Godavari River, making it an important pilgrimage destination in Hindu tradition. The temple's architecture showcases intricate carvings and the famous "Kund" (water tank), which is considered sacred and where pilgrims perform ritualistic bathing. The temple has been an important center of worship for centuries and has been renovated and maintained by various rulers throughout history. The Godavari River, originating near the temple, is considered the second holiest river in India after the Ganges, and pilgrims often undertake the sacred journey of circumambulating the entire river from its source to its confluence with the Arabian Sea.

Significance

Trimbakeshwar is the eighth Jyotirlinga and is unique as it is the source point of the sacred Godavari River. The temple holds immense significance in Hindu pilgrimage traditions, particularly for those undertaking the sacred journey of circumambulating the Godavari.
