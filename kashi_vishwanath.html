Location

Kashi Vishwanath is located on the banks of the sacred Ganges River in Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh. The temple is situated in the heart of the city, which is considered one of the most sacred pilgrimage destinations in Hindu tradition and the spiritual capital of India.

History

The Kashi Vishwanath Temple is one of the most important pilgrimage centers in Hinduism and is dedicated to Lord Shiva in his manifestation as Vishwanath (Lord of the Universe). Varanasi, also known as Kashi or Banaras, has been a major center of Hindu learning and spirituality for over 2,500 years. The current temple structure was built by Maharani Ahilyabai Holkar of Indore in 1780, as the previous temple was destroyed during various invasions. The temple's golden spire, added in 1839, is one of the most recognizable symbols of Hindu spiritual architecture. The temple is located near the Ganges River, and the sacred ghats (steps) surrounding the area are where millions of pilgrims perform their rituals and take holy dips. The city of Varanasi is unique as it is believed to be the place where Lord Shiva himself resides, making it a primary destination for pilgrims seeking spiritual enlightenment. The temple remains the beating heart of Hindu pilgrimage traditions despite the challenges and invasions it has faced throughout its long history.

Significance

Kashi Vishwanath is the seventh Jyotirlinga and is the most visited pilgrimage site among all Jyotirlingas. The temple holds immense spiritual significance as it is believed that those who see the Shivling here or bathe in the Ganges at this location attain salvation and liberation from the cycle of rebirth.
