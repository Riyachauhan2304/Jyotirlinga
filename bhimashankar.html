Location

Bhimashankar is located on the Sahyadri mountain range in Maharashtra. The temple is situated at an elevation where the sacred Bhima River originates, surrounded by verdant forests and wildlife.

History

The Bhimashankar Temple is dedicated to Lord Shiva and is named after the demon Bhima, who is believed to have been a devotee of Shiva. According to mythology, Lord Shiva killed the demon Bhima and established his presence at this location. The temple's architecture reflects the Hemadpanthi style of construction, characterized by black stone structures and intricate carvings. The temple has been an important center of worship and pilgrimage for centuries, attracting devotees from across the region. The surrounding area is home to diverse flora and fauna, including the endangered black giant squirrel found in the Western Ghats. The temple is located near a wildlife sanctuary, adding to its natural and spiritual significance. The Bhima River, originating near the temple, is considered sacred and its waters are believed to have healing properties.

Significance

Bhimashankar is the sixth Jyotirlinga and is unique as it is the only Jyotirlinga located on a mountain peak. The temple offers a unique combination of spiritual and natural beauty, making it a significant pilgrimage destination in western India.
