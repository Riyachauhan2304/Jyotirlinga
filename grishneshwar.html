Location

Grishneshwar is located on the banks of the Godavari River near Aurangabad in Maharashtra. The temple is situated close to the UNESCO World Heritage site of the Ellora Caves, one of the most important historical and religious sites in India.

History

The Grishneshwar Temple, also known as Ghrishneshwar, is the last and twelfth Jyotirlinga in the traditional list. The temple is dedicated to Lord Shiva and is located near the magnificent Ellora Caves, which contain ancient Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain temples carved into rock. According to mythology, the temple was established by Adi Shankaracharya, the great philosopher and saint who is credited with reviving Hinduism in India. The temple's architecture reflects the Hemadpanthi style of construction with intricate stone carvings and ornate decorations. The temple has been an important pilgrimage destination for centuries and has been maintained and renovated by various devotees and rulers throughout history. The proximity to the Ellora Caves adds to the historical and spiritual significance of the location, as the caves contain examples of ancient Hindu art and architecture spanning over 1,000 years. The temple stands as a testament to the enduring spiritual tradition of worshipping Lord Shiva in various forms across different regions and time periods.

Significance

Grishneshwar is the twelfth and final Jyotirlinga in the traditional pilgrimage list. The temple holds immense significance as it is believed to be the last of the 12 sacred Jyotirlingas established by Lord Shiva himself, and pilgrims often complete their spiritual journey with a visit to this temple.
